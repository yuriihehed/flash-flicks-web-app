Learning Styles

Not all study techniques are created equal. Discover different approaches to find one that matches your style!

Explore Popular Study Methods

Memory Palaces & Visualization

Memory palaces involve associating information with specific locations in a familiar space—used by memory champions for complex memorization tasks.

To use this method, visualize a familiar place and place key information at specific spots. As you mentally walk through it, recall each piece at the assigned location.

Best for: Visual learners and those needing to memorize large amounts of information.

Leitner System

The Leitner System sorts flashcards into "boxes" based on how well you remember each card. Cards you recall correctly move to a higher box, and mistakes return to Box 1.

Review lower-numbered boxes more frequently than higher ones, ensuring you spend more time on the cards you find most difficult.

Best for: Learners who want a simple, time-efficient method.

Spaced Repetition (SRS)

Spaced repetition involves reviewing flashcards at increasing intervals over time, proven by research to enhance long-term memory.

Tools like Anki schedule reviews automatically, ensuring you study just before forgetting—reinforcing knowledge efficiently.

Best for: Students aiming for long-term retention.

Peer Quizzing

Studying with friends brings interactive and engaging elements into learning. Ask each other questions and discuss the answers to deepen understanding.

Explaining concepts aloud can strengthen retention and highlight areas that need further review.

Best for: Social learners who thrive on discussion and active engagement.

Traditional Self-Testing

A straightforward approach: read the front of the flashcard, try to recall the answer, and check the back. Repeat until you've mastered all the cards.

This simple method is effective for reinforcing recall through direct repetition.

Best for: Students preferring a direct, structured study approach.

Writing & Speaking Responses

Writing your answers forces you to structure your thoughts, while speaking them aloud can enhance auditory learning.

Consistently expressing responses in words (written or spoken) strengthens understanding and retention.

Best for: Learners who retain information better by articulating concepts.
